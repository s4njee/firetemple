## How it works

Story 6.8: solid boundaries in the stable-fluids solver (`src/gpu/sim/fluid.ts`), so that hot gas rising from under a page meets the page's elements as walls. A burner below the content puts heat and smoke into the fluid; buoyancy lifts the heat; the plume splits round the cards, headings and buttons, curls over their top edges, and the smoke it carries pools under them before it spills round their ends.

**The fluid.** The solver at 128 cells on the long side (dye four times finer), MacCormack advection for both velocity and dye, vorticity confinement 4 / s against a damping of 0.6 / s, `open` edges (the gas leaves through the top and sides, and air is drawn in from below). Dye r is heat, dye b is smoke. Buoyancy reads the dye through `buoyancyWeights` [1, 0, -0.05, 0] (heat lifts, smoke weighs a little), and the dissipation is per channel: heat cools at `cooling` (0.6 / s), smoke thins at `smoke fade` (0.2 / s). There is no combustion model here (no fuel, no reaction); that is the `fire-fluid` effect of stories 6.2 to 6.7. This effect drives the solver directly with hot buoyant gas, as the `stable-fluids` validation scene does.

**Heat in.** The burner is a line just above the bottom edge (`BURNER_Y` = 0.985 of the height), `burner width` of the canvas wide, cut into tongues about 0.1 canvas heights apart (`burnerTongues`). A tongue that a solid covers is left out, and when solids cover the whole span (the `backdrop` layout's article runs off the bottom of the canvas) the free places on the rest of the line are used instead, so the fire comes up beside the element rather than vanishing under it. Each tongue is a dye splat of heat and smoke plus a small upward push, breathing on its own slow clock (0.8 to 1.6 rad / s) and wandering sideways by a third of the spacing, so the columns never line up. A slow side wind comes and goes. Stray embers (`createAmbientIgnition`, a Poisson process on the simulation clock) drop puffs of heat in the lower part of the canvas, so the scene is never dead even with the burner at 0 (`embers only`). The pointer goes through the shared ignition layer (`createPointerIgnition`): a press or a drag adds heat and smoke along the segments the pointer covered since the last frame; a hover adds nothing. The `drive` parameter (`createExcitation`) raises the burner by up to 60% of its calm level and the ember rate by up to 100%; it never touches the simulation clock.

**The solids.** Three sources, one path:

- **The page** (`solids: page`, the default). `trackDomRects` measures the rounded rectangles of the elements marked `data-ft-obstacle` in canvas uv every frame (one `getBoundingClientRect` each; computed style is cached). `shapeFromRect` turns each into a rounded-box shape in image heights, with the element's velocity when the tracker saw its edges move. `createObstacleShapes` keeps the shape table (a uniform buffer) and draws it into the solver's obstacle mask (`r8unorm`, one texel per cell, solid where the cell centre is inside a shape) with one small render pass, only when the table changed or the grid was re-created.
- **Stand-ins** when no element overlaps the canvas (tests, the bare canvas, a gallery thumbnail): a heading bar, a wide plate and a row of three cards (`standInRects`; a portrait variant for tall canvases), so the effect is never a fire with nothing to go round.
- **SDF test shapes** (`card row`, `circle`, `slanted plate`): analytic shapes from `obstacle-shapes.ts` that replace the page, for judging the flow round a solid on its own. The slanted plate rises to the right; the gas slides up its underside and spills off the high end.

**When the layout changes** (a resize, a breakpoint, an element added or moved by more than a quarter of a cell) the table is replaced and the mask re-rasterised in the next frame, and nothing else happens: the grids are not re-created, the cells the new mask covers lose their gas (the solver holds solid cells at zero velocity and zero dye every step), the cells it uncovers are born still and clear, and the flow everywhere else carries on. `tests/epic6-c.test.ts` moves a rectangle and checks exactly that. While an element moves (a card scrolling over a fixed canvas), the shapes carry its velocity and the moving-boundary pass of `obstacle-shapes.ts` is installed in `fluid.hooks.forces`, so the element pushes the gas ahead of it; the pass is removed again once nothing moves, and the shapes are re-sent once with zero velocity.

**Drawing** (`scene.wgsl`, one pass at canvas size). Heat is emission through `ramp_fire` of 1 - exp(-0.9 heat): red, orange and a yellow core near the burner, above 1 so it blooms. Smoke has real coverage, 1 - exp(-opacity × smoke), lit by a dim ambient and by the heat inside it. The solids are cut with their exact outline from the same shape table the mask came from, which hides the mask's staircase. Inside a solid nothing is drawn but firelight on its edge: the heat one and a half cells outside, along the outward normal, lights a band a third of a cell deep inside the outline (`firelight on solids`). Over a page that band lies on the element itself, so a card's lower edge glows where the flames lick it. The shape table is walked only near a solid: the mask's bilinear tap is zero more than a cell away from every solid cell.

**Over the page.** It is an overlay (`OVERLAY_PARAMS`, luminance alpha). The mock layouts put their content above the canvas, on mostly opaque plates, and the fire has to be drawn round those plates and over the page beside them, so while the solids are real elements the effect raises its canvas above its siblings (`position: relative; z-index: 5`), as `dom-fuel` does (`canvas above content`). Inside an element only the edge firelight and the bloom are drawn, so the element's own text stays readable.

**A still frame is already a fire.** Before the first frame (and after the grids are re-created) the effect runs 2.5 s of burning, one step per command buffer (queued splats go through one buffer, so steps that each need their own splats cannot share a submit). Under reduced motion the held frame shows flames under and round the solids, not an empty canvas.

## Cost

GPU time, measured on a quiet GPU (Apple M1 Max, 1920 × 1080, default settings, simulation and tail included): 1.4 ms per frame on Dawn, where the tail alone reads 0.7 ms; and 4.85 ms for one frame in Chrome 155 at 60 fps (median of 3 loads, 4.81 to 4.99), which includes the browser's timing floor. The gap between the two is larger for these effects than for the single-pass ones, because a fluid step is many small dispatches. Both are inside the Tier B budget of 6 ms on this machine; no integrated GPU or phone has been measured. How the cost moves with grid size and pressure solver is in `docs/studies/epic-6.md` (story 6.10). Earlier indicative HUD readings in headless Chrome at 1920 × 1080 on an Apple M1 Max at the defaults (the stand-in solids), with other agents using the GPU: three runs of `npm run browser-check` (view `hud-1080p`, three loads each) gave medians of 4.2, 4.8 and 3.4 ms (single loads from 3.3 to 5.5 ms), so 55 to 90% of the Tier B budget of 6 ms. In the run that read 3.4 ms, `stable-fluids` (same solver, same grid) read 2.6 ms. The readings move by more than the difference between the two, so where the extra time goes (the display pass walks the shape table near the solids; the burner has more splats) is not settled. No integrated GPU or phone has been measured, so the tier is provisional; `velocity cells` 96 is the setting to try first on a slower GPU.

Counted from the code, at the defaults:

- **Simulation:** 128 × 72 cells, dye 512 × 288. One step per frame at 60 Hz: MacCormack velocity advection (2 dispatches over 129 × 73 faces), curl, curl blur and forces (3), divergence, 30 Jacobi iterations and the projection (32 over 128 × 72), MacCormack dye advection (2 over 147 thousand texels). About 40 dispatches a step. Splats: up to 13 tongues of dye and force, the wind, embers and the pointer, applied in two small passes before the step.
- **Solids:** the mask pass (one fullscreen draw over 128 × 72 texels, each testing every shape) only when the layout changed; the moving-boundary pass (one draw over the velocity texture per step) only while an element moves. `trackDomRects` costs one `getBoundingClientRect` per marked element per frame on the CPU.
- **Display:** one pass over the canvas: one filtered dye tap and one mask tap per pixel; near a solid (within a cell of it) the shape table is walked once (a few dozen ALU per shape) and inside a solid one more dye tap. Then the HDR tail (bloom and output), which at 1080p costs more than the solver.
- **Memory:** about 4 MB for the solver, 25 MB in all at 1080p with the tail (the HUD read 24.9 MB).
- **What scales it:** the solver scales with `velocity cells` squared and not with the canvas; the display pass with the canvas and, near the solids, with the number of marked elements (at most 64).

## Pitfalls

- **The burner has to fit under the elements.** With the burner a little above the bottom (0.95 of the height), the hero layout's copy plate, which comes down to 0.97, sat on top of it: the tongues under it were inside a solid and lost, and the plate showed only a glow line at its foot. In the `cards` layout the featured plate covers almost all of the canvas's lower half and the effect showed nothing else. At 0.985 the gas collects in the gap under the plate and escapes round its ends, which is the look this effect is for. In the `backdrop` layout the article covers the whole burner and runs off the canvas's bottom edge: the effect first showed nothing that moved (the browser check reported the view as still), and the burner now moves its tongues to the free places beside such an element. In `cards` the canvas is one small card whose plate leaves gaps of only a few pixels at its sides, so the fire there is a glow under the plate and thin flames up its sides and over its top corners.
- **The mask is a staircase of cells; the drawing is not.** A cell is solid when its centre is inside a shape, so the wall the gas sees is off the drawn outline by up to half a cell (about 7 px at 1080p and 128 cells), and the dye fades to zero over the last half cell before a wall because the bilinear tap reads the solid cell's zero. Near a wall that reads as a thin dark gap between the flame and the card. A gap between two elements narrower than about two cells is closed or open depending on where the cell centres fall; at the default grid on a 1080p canvas that is about 30 CSS px.
- **Raising the canvas.** The fire round an element has to be drawn over the page, so the canvas goes above the content while the solids are real elements. That is a change outside the effect's own pixels, made only for the page set and undone on `destroy`; a shell option for an overlay layer would be cleaner (the same note as `dom-fuel`).
- **Moving solids need their velocity cleared.** The tracker reports motion only while an element moves; a table left with the last velocity would keep pushing the gas. The effect re-sends the shapes once with zero velocity when the motion stops.
- **Steps that need their own splats need their own submit.** The warm-up first queued its splats and ran 150 steps in one encoder, and every step saw the last step's splats: one `writeBuffer` before a submit lands before any step runs. One command buffer per step fixes it, at the price of 150 small submits once.
- **Open edges and long runs.** Every preset was run for 30 s headless: the largest velocity stayed under 1.1 canvas heights a second (the cap at this grid is 5) and no field went non-finite.
- **Thermals in 2D roll up into pairs of eyes.** A small hot puff rises as a vortex pair, which looks like a pair of glowing eyes when the puff is small and hot. The embers are wide (0.045 canvas heights) and lukewarm for that reason, and are given no push of their own.
- **WGSL.** Scene names start with `fo_`; the obstacle chunk and the table come in through `OBSTACLE_CHUNKS`, since `obstacle-shapes.wgsl` is neither beside the effect nor in `src/wgsl/`.

## When to use it

A landing page, a 404 or a game menu whose cards and headings sit in a rising fire that knows where they are: it parts round them, licks their lower edges and pools under them. It is Tier B (provisionally; measured only on an Apple M1 Max), needs compute shaders (no WebGL2 path), and reads over both page tones: over a light page the flames are saturated red and orange ink with grey smoke, and the elements stay clear. Flash: `low`, measured: at the defaults over 8 s the busiest cell of the picture completes one flash (two counting saturated red, three in `round obstacle`), no area large enough to count ever flashes, and the frame's mean luminance moves by under 1% of white; every preset is under the threshold (`tests/flash.test.ts`).

For fire that only climbs the elements and needs no flow, `dom-fuel` (Epic 5) is cheaper (Tier A) and has no solver. For a combustion model with fuel and soot, use `fire-fluid` (6.2 to 6.7); its solids would come from the same `obstacle-shapes.ts` path. Do not use either over content that must be read while it burns: the canvas sits on top.
